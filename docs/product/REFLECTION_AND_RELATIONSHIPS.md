# Reflection and Relationships

**Status:** Approved
**Builds:** 029–030

## Product outcome

Koravik now closes the loop between a real-life Quest and the Healing Home. A player may resolve a Quest in more than one honest way. The Healing Home then preserves a visible, explainable change without converting life into points, currency, or performance.

## Reflection boundary

- Quest owns the real commitment and its resolution.
- Chronicle owns saved personal reflection.
- Worlds receive only permitted minimized facts.
- Healing Home may display a derived change or keepsake, but does not become the owner of the Quest, Chronicle entry, or World reaction.
- Materialization is idempotent by source and change kind.

## Home changes

Quest resolutions and World reactions may create calm environmental acknowledgements such as a changed Quest Board, warmer firelight, or a small keepsake. Released, paused, and deferred Quests remain valid outcomes and are never framed as moral failure.

## Relationship model

Relationships are account-scoped narrative continuity. They contain:

- a named character;
- a qualitative state such as new, familiar, or trusted;
- durable memories with source provenance;
- a readable shared-history screen.

Familiarity is an internal presentation aid, not affection, reputation, obedience, or social rank. There are no streak penalties, inactivity losses, jealousy systems, or relationship purchases.

## Initial character

The Caretaker is the first supported relationship. The Caretaker remembers World reactions already approved for Epic Ordinary. It does not independently inspect private Quest notes, Chronicle prose, Companion memory, Health records, Beacon attendance, or Gather communication.

## Future integration earmarks

- Beacon may contribute a minimized fact such as voluntary community participation.
- Gather may contribute an approved shared-action fact.
- Health may contribute only deliberately approved abstractions such as preparing for care or making space for recovery.
- Each source remains the owner of its records and consent controls.
