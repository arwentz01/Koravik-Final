# Koravik Visual Information Architecture

**Status:** Approved
**Version:** 1.0
**Effective build:** 021

## Purpose

Every capability must have one understandable visual home. Features may surface contextually elsewhere, but their full lifecycle and controls belong to the owner listed here.

## Primary places

- **Hearth:** orientation, one next step, return summaries, and bounded composition. Hearth links outward and never becomes a complete control center.
- **Quests:** real-life intentions, recurrence, steps, milestones, occurrence lifecycle, and completion.
- **Chronicle:** personal entries, approved reflections, provenance, tags, archive, restore, and eligible deletion.
- **Worlds:** catalog, installation, fictional continuation, permissions preview, World State, and reactions.
- **Companion:** requests for help, proposals, clarification, approval, lifecycle, selected context, and approved Companion memory.

## Utilities

- **Search:** authorized discovery across owners.
- **Notifications:** explainable attention items and preferences.

Utilities never become source owners.

## Account and trust

These surfaces live beneath the account menu rather than competing with daily-use navigation:

- **Koravik guide:** map of capabilities and ownership;
- **Settings:** identity, appearance, accessibility, time, and low-risk preferences;
- **Privacy:** consent, source, recipient, purpose, last use, and revocation;
- **Audit activity:** append-only consequential history;
- **Security:** password and session protection;
- **Data controls:** export, account closure, cancellation, and retention information.

## Contextual surfacing rules

- Hearth may preview but not manage full source lifecycle.
- Notifications link to the owning source.
- Search results identify the owner.
- Companion proposals name the destination owner.
- Worlds explain the real-life fact category without displaying unauthorized District details.
- Settings links to consequence-specific surfaces instead of duplicating their controls.

## Responsive hierarchy

Desktop uses persistent primary navigation, utilities, and an account menu. Mobile uses a single accessible menu toggle preserving the same hierarchy and actions. No feature may exist only through hover, horizontal scrolling, or an unlabeled icon.

## Expansion rule

A new capability must identify its visual home before implementation. When no existing home fits, product review must decide whether it is a new owner, a utility, an account/trust control, or contextual content.
