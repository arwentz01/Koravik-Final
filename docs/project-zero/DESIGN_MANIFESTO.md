# Design Manifesto

## Koravik should feel calm, warm, purposeful, and alive

The interface should help people orient themselves without creating urgency that does not exist. It should feel human rather than clinical, playful without becoming childish, and expressive without becoming noisy.

## One primary question per screen

Every major surface should make its immediate purpose understandable. Secondary information may support the decision, but it must not compete with it.

## Progressive simplicity

As Koravik gains capabilities, the everyday experience should become clearer. More Districts, Worlds, integrations, and Companion intelligence must not produce a denser home screen or a more confusing navigation model.

## Resumption over punishment

Life is interrupted. Koravik must expect missed days, unfinished work, changing priorities, illness, grief, and ordinary distraction. Returning should provide orientation and a humane next step, never shame.

## Explain important behavior

Nothing consequential should feel magical. World reactions, Companion suggestions, reminders, permissions, and automated processing must expose why they occurred and what source informed them.

## Agency before automation

Automation may reduce friction, but it may not quietly decide what matters. Companion and system services may suggest, summarize, prepare, and remind. Consequential changes require explicit authority and an auditable owning service.

## Encourage life outside the product

Koravik is successful when it helps a person act, connect, recover, remember, or grow away from the screen. It must not use addictive mechanics, artificial scarcity, or anxiety to increase engagement.

## Accessibility is design

Keyboard operation, readable contrast, understandable language, reduced-motion support, adaptable layouts, visible focus, assistive-technology semantics, and forgiving interaction are product requirements rather than final-stage compliance tasks.

## Worlds add meaning without distorting reality

Worlds may interpret approved real-life facts into narrative consequence. They must not edit source truth, expose sensitive details unnecessarily, or make fiction a prerequisite for practical participation.

## Hearth is curated, not crowded

Hearth should answer: What matters now? It composes District-owned facts, World continuation, and approved proposals. It does not become the owner of those records and must resist widget accumulation that obscures orientation.
